## What it needs

Four conditions. Miss one and you have choreography, a crowd, rumor, or a blob.

1. **Local rules, not a conductor.** No manager particle. No orchestrator species. Coordination does not fall out of making each agent smarter.
2. **Difference that holds.** Separate is a rule, not decoration. Five species. Not five colors. Physics differ; paint follows. Hermes lichen `#7cc47a` is not Honey gold `#e0b25a`.
3. **A channel that is not the core.** Honey is the only relationship species. Exploring may cross. Returned may land. Alignment without a crossing is parallel play.
4. **A bound.** Shell marks the edge. Unbounded cohesion is a blob. Shell does not rewrite the core.

## Sources (evidence, not decoration)

- Reynolds (1987), *Flocks, herds and schools* — three local rules suffice: separation, alignment, cohesion. No scripted path. No leader.
- Ballerini et al. (2008) — starling interaction is **topological, not metric**. Each bird tracks a handful of neighbors (~6–7), not everyone inside a radius. Density can explode and the flock still holds.
- Cavagna et al. (2010) — correlation length **scales with flock size**. A turn on one edge is statistically tied to the far edge. Effective perception exceeds actual neighborhood.
- Cavagna, Giardina et al. — information in real flocks **propagates** (linear, barely damped) rather than diffusing, because of alignment plus inertia.
- Anthropic multi-agent systems (2026) — coordination does not naturally emerge from stronger individual intelligence or alignment. The physics *between* peers is the work.

### What emergence needs

It is picky. It fails in predictable ways.

**Local rules, not a manager.**  
Hire one agent to coordinate the others and you have choreography. Choreography can be beautiful. It is not emergence. Making each agent smarter does not produce coordination either. You need the physics *between* them.

**Difference that holds.**  
If every part is the same color and the same physics, you have a crowd, not a system. Separation is not a nice-to-have. It is one of the three rules. Homogenize the parts and the whole disappears.

**A channel that is not the core.**  
Alignment without a crossing is parallel play. A crossing with no stamp is rumor. Something has to move between the parts. Something else has to refuse to move.

**A bound.**  
Unbounded cohesion is a blob. The membrane is what keeps a murmuration a murmuration.

Starlings add a quieter finding. Each bird does not track everyone inside a radius. It tracks a handful of neighbors — about six or seven — no matter how dense the flock becomes. The interaction is topological, not metric. Density can explode and the group still holds, because the rule is *these neighbors*, not *this many meters*.

The strange property that follows: a turn on one edge is statistically tied to birds on the far edge. Correlation length scales with the size of the flock. Each bird’s effective perception is larger than its actual neighborhood. The crossing gives the whole a range no individual earned.
