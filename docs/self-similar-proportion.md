# Self-Similar Proportion

Self-similar proportion is the second foundational dynamic of Living Spiral Systems.

## Core Idea

A pattern is self-similar when its essential logic remains recognizable as it moves across scales — from individual to team, from team to organization, from organization to larger system.

This is not rigid uniformity. It is coherence of structure and relationship across levels.

## Why It Matters

Many transformation efforts produce insights or solutions that work at one scale but break when scaled. Self-similar design reduces this fracture by ensuring that the generative logic of a solution can be expressed at multiple levels without losing integrity.

## Design Implications

- Prefer patterns that can be expressed simply at small scale and still make sense at larger scale.
- When designing interventions, ask: “What is the essential relationship or rhythm here, and how would it look one level up and one level down?”
- Avoid solutions whose success depends on conditions that cannot be replicated across the system.

## Relationship to Rhythm

Rhythm (the 3-6-9 cycle) and proportion reinforce each other. A coherent rhythm applied at multiple scales produces nested spirals rather than disconnected initiatives. This nesting is a primary source of long-term coherence.
