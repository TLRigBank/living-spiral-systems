## 1. Situation

An Arizona wholesale grower of cactus, agave, aloe, and other desert-adapted material. Multiple yards. Hundreds of taxa. Will-call and delivery. Sales people must know plants and house rules. Knowledge transfer is the bottleneck.

The operator designed learning materials, maps, and onboarding against a 650-species load. No claimed revenue lift, headcount change, or that the grower bought this Diagnostic.

**Public problem:** wholesale nursery + 600–650 taxa + new reps + several yards = onboarding cannot live in one senior’s head.

## 2. Current work / waste

- New-rep first 30 days: species dump with no halt.
- Substitution: a chat-shaped plant-ID agent invents a lookalike.
- Multi-yard SOPs that pretend yards are one organism.
- Visit / no-end-client-pricing rules living as tribal knowledge.
- Extra dashboards that do not change will-call pull discipline.

## 3. Architecture

1. **Spine** — visit rule, no end-client pricing, substitution honesty, will-call time promise. Agents may not rewrite these.
2. **Onboarding loop** — 30 high-frequency taxa first; halt rare specimens a hire will not sell this month.
3. **Tools** — buy photo + existing availability. Do not build a catalog chatbot or a second inventory.

RAF sentence: the expensive mistake is an agent that answers faster than the spine allows.

## 4. 90-day loop

Days 1–30: Spine page + 30 taxa + one yard map.
Days 31–60: substitution pairs for those 30; skip list for rare taxa.
Days 61–90: second-yard differences only. No new software.

Do not invent a percent lift. First check is “rules page used, yes/no” and “chatbot project opened, yes/no.”

## 5. Will-not-build

Catalog chatbot; full-taxon day-one curriculum; extra dashboard; public price bot; one SOP for several yards; First Root (wrong organism).

Allowed to conclude: maps + 30-taxon core + the availability tool they already have.
